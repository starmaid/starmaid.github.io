I am frequently haunted by a spectre. Not one of supernatural origin, but the thought that I picked the wrong connector for a project. 

## What I Did Before

When I was in high school, most of my projects used screw terminals. The first connector set I bought was Dupont, the same width as arduino and raspberry pi pins. Then I bought a set of JST-SM (almost the same as Dupont, but has the clip), and nailed down a technique:

1. Crimp the cable normally (I just used pliers for this because I didn't buy the tool)
2. Put a drop of solder on the back of the connector
3. Insert all the pins into the plastic housing
4. Put a glob of hot glue around the back of the housing for strain relief

This can be seen on a few projects. 

![picture of some project that used a dupont or JST]()

For power I started using barrel jacks for 12V and 9V projects. The most annoying shit is figuring out which barrel jack SPECIFICALLY a device uses.

- 2.1x5.5mm
- 2.5x5.5mm
- Any infinite random connector

![show a picture of the two barrel jacks]()

These also were frequently used for 18V, 24V, and other random voltages in projects. I started running out of supplies and matching connectors. It sucked.

### Battery Side Note

I use XT-30, -60, and -90 on all my LiPo projects. Those connectors are awesome. But unfortunately those have a different use case than the things I'm trying to power here. I did have a quick thought of unifying them, but that seemed like a lot of trouble.

## A Step

I realized I could just use USB for power to my 5V projects. That was huge, I bought a pack of connectors that had four easy solder pads for Micro-USC and USB-C. These even let me do data through some of the connectors and it was totally awesome.

![picture of some custom USB connectors]()

However, newer power supplies stopped working in specific cases. If I just used the power rails from a USB-C connector, some wall supplies would not supply current. This was because the device was not negotiating anything, and it wouldn't fall back to just 5V 2A. This vexed me.

## Finally

So I bought [some boards that you solder some jumper pads](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CPDRY45Q) and it negotiates the voltage you want. I can make a super short adapter to whatever the target device needs, and then just run with it. 

![picture of a short cable]()

This can be paired with nice type-C power supplies for really efficient and compact project assemblies. 

From the reviews, looks like some people actually glue the board inside the device they are converting to USB-C, which I might do for certain things. Interesting idea.

![protogen head with power bank]()

If the project needs to be portable, little power banks like [this](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DZ1TGX6T) are perfect. They can deliver any voltage and have so much power its incredible.

![raspberry pi server rack]()

If the project is a static install that has multiple devices, like a bunch of RPi's and a network switch, the whole thing can be powered off of [something like this](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DNSRXRFT) which has four USB-PD outputs.

## Type C Cables

The unsolved annoying problem for C to C cables: at a glance, it is impossible to distinguish between

- Power-only
- USB 2
- USB 3
- any subtly broken cable

I should get something that can test the connection and tell me if individual connectors are broken. Then I could like spray-paint them or something, and update them as my cables degrade. I had a super frustrating moment trying to flash a Meshtastic node with a cable that seemed to have data, but stopped working once the transfer started. [Oh, only $9 on Amazon?](https://www.amazon.com/gp/product/B0GD218NPJ) Might have to grab that.
